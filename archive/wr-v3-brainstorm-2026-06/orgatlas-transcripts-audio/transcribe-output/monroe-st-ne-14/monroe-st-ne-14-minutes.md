# Meeting Minutes: Monroe St NE 14

## Purpose

Clarify what White Rabbit is actually selling, who the first buyer/user should be, and whether the product should focus on enriching existing lead lists, generating boutique lead lists, or helping individual sales reps work faster.

## Key Context

Matt and Lee discussed a product tension from prior demos and prototype work. White Rabbit can produce useful targeted lists, but the team has been drifting between several possible products: a ZoomInfo alternative, a list-enrichment layer, a boutique lead-generation service, a search-bar workflow, and a sales-rep productivity tool.

Matt argued that White Rabbit should not compete with ZoomInfo on volume or infrastructure. ZoomInfo can scrape and maintain massive datasets, automate workflows, and sell to enterprise teams. White Rabbit's advantage is more likely to be targeted, high-value, boutique work for users who cannot afford, do not know how to use, or do not have access to enterprise sales-intelligence tools.

The group also discussed data ownership. If a company pays for a ZoomInfo list, an individual employee may not have the right to take that list elsewhere for enrichment or outside use. That makes the buyer/user choice important.

## Decisions

- Do not position White Rabbit as a direct ZoomInfo replacement.
- Treat "source-assisted enrichment" and "autonomous boutique lead generation" as separate product wedges.
- Start by choosing one wedge and one customer type rather than building for every possible lead-generation use case.
- The product needs a clear North Star: who is using it, who pays for it, what workflow it improves, and what success looks like.
- The strongest near-term value may be helping an individual sales rep or small sales operation create or enrich better lists.
- Future demos should begin with discovery of the customer's current workflow, pain, cost, and success metric before showing a generic product.

The group agreed that White Rabbit's value depends heavily on expectations. A technically good list can still disappoint if the customer expects a different level of volume, conversion, or certainty. This is why the buyer/user/workflow definition matters.
