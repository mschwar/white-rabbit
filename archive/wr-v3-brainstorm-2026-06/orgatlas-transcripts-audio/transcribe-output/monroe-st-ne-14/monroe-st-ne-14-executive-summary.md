# Executive Summary: Monroe St NE 14

## Summary

The conversation centered on narrowing White Rabbit from a broad lead-generation idea into a specific product wedge that can be sold and validated. The group repeatedly returned to a core constraint: White Rabbit should not try to become or replace ZoomInfo. ZoomInfo wins on volume, data access, search depth, workflow automation, and capital. White Rabbit's opportunity is boutique, targeted, and workflow-aware: create or enrich higher-value lead lists for people who do not have good access, training, time, or usable workflows.

Three product directions were discussed. The first is source-assisted enrichment: ingest an existing ZoomInfo or prospecting list, enrich it, score it, and help a sales manager or rep prioritize the best leads. The second is autonomous boutique lead generation: let a smaller business or operator request a specific lead segment and receive a curated list. The third, which emerged near the end, is a sales-rep-first tool: create or enrich lists for an individual rep who knows half their contacts are bad and wants better leads to bring into their company.

The strongest strategic point was that the team needs to define the buyer, user, workflow, and success metric before building more. A list that looks valuable to Thomas as a sales rep may not look valuable to a CEO or sales manager. The team needs to know what current tools cost, what current workflows look like, what outcomes matter, and why the previous test list created excitement.

Matt and Lee also watched or referenced a ZoomInfo YouTube review that described advanced search filters, scoops, intent signals, web-visitor tracking, data cleansing, workflows, and high per-seat pricing. That reference reinforced both sides of the strategy: ZoomInfo is powerful and expensive, and many smaller or less sophisticated users either cannot afford it or cannot use it well.

## Bottom Line

The likely near-term wedge is not "better ZoomInfo." It is a simpler, boutique tool or service that creates or enriches useful lead lists for a sharply defined sales user. The next work should be customer/workflow discovery and internal dogfooding, not broad product expansion.

## Key Risks

- Building several product ideas at once before choosing a North Star.
- Competing against ZoomInfo or similar platforms where incumbents have better data access and capital.
- Burning trust if a paid list is technically high-confidence but the customer does not perceive it as valuable.
- Confusing buyer, user, and beneficiary: CEO, sales manager, and sales rep may value different things.
- Data ownership risk if individual reps enrich or reuse employer-owned ZoomInfo/prospect lists.
- Over-indexing on Thomas's excitement without knowing whether his boss, company, or a paying customer would react the same way.
