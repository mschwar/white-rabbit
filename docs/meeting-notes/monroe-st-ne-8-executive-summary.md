## Summary

The group aligned on turning the tool into a focused lead-generation sandbox for the first demo and internal testing. The key decision was to avoid presenting it as an unrestricted AI assistant. Instead, it should be capped, lead-gen-specific, and practical for sales workflows.

The agreed starting package is 10 queries, 100 leads or rows per query, and 1,000 total output rows. The tool should redirect out-of-scope prompts back toward lead-generation inputs such as job title, vertical, industry, and related criteria.

Matt committed to getting this guarded version into Lee and Thomas's hands by end of day Friday. Lee and Thomas will use it directly so the team can learn from real usage, lead quality, cost behavior, and customer-facing workflow gaps.

Strategically, the product is being positioned as a simpler, more conversational alternative to list-building tools like ZoomInfo and DiscoverOrg. Thomas sees strong sales potential if the tool stays easy to use and produces clean, validated lists.

The team deferred broader B2C use cases and advanced data-source ideas until after the B2B demo path is working. Immediate priorities are the Scotty/demo use case, B2B lead generation, export usability, prompt guardrails, and basic cost discovery.

## Key Risks

- Prompt abuse or off-scope usage if the tool behaves like a general AI assistant.
- Unclear unit economics until real capped runs are measured.
- Need for clear client-data isolation as more customers are added.
- Reset/admin workflow for internal users who exhaust test limits.
- Overextending into B2C or broader intelligence use cases before the B2B demo path is stable.

## Bottom Line

The meeting converted the product direction from broad possibility into an actionable first sandbox: limited queries, limited rows, lead-gen-only outputs, Friday internal access, and empirical learning from real use.
